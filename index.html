Glass Buying Guide

Types of Glass & Where to Use Them

Choosing the right glass matters for safety, privacy and looks. Here is a quick guide to the glass types we work with every day.

Toughened Glass

Heat-treated safety glass, roughly 4–5× stronger than normal glass. If broken, it crumbles into small blunt pieces.

Best for
Doors, partitions, showers, railings
Typical thickness
5–19mm

Laminated Glass

Two or more sheets bonded with a PVB interlayer. Stays in place if cracked, reduces noise and blocks most UV.

Best for
Railings, skylights, facades, acoustic rooms
Typical thickness
6.38–17.52mm

Frosted Glass

Acid-etched, sandblasted or film finish that lets light through while blocking clear views.

Best for
Bathrooms, cabin privacy bands, doors
Typical thickness
5–12mm

Fluted / Reeded Glass

Vertical ribbed texture that softens views and adds a premium, light-catching look.

Best for
Pooja doors, wardrobes, partitions
Typical thickness
4–10mm

Tinted & Reflective

Grey, bronze or blue body tint, or a reflective coating, to cut glare and solar heat.

Best for
Windows, facades, wardrobe shutters
Typical thickness
5–12mm

Lacquered (Back-Painted)

Opaque colour coating on the back face. Glossy, hygienic and easy to wipe clean.

Best for
Kitchen backsplash, shutters, wall cladding
Typical thickness
4–6mm

DGU / Insulated Glass

Two panes separated by a sealed air or argon gap that insulates against heat and noise.

Best for
UPVC & aluminium windows, road-facing rooms
Typical thickness
20–28mm unit

Bent / Curved Glass

Glass heated and formed to a radius, then toughened or laminated for strength.

Best for
Curved partitions, stair railings, showrooms
Typical thickness
6–12mm

Quick Reference

Which Glass Thickness Do I Need?

Recommended glass type and thickness by application
Application Recommended glass Thickness
UPVC & aluminium windows Clear, tinted or DGU 5–6mm single / 20–24mm DGU
Wardrobe & kitchen shutters Lacquered, fluted, tinted, mirror 4–6mm
Shower enclosure Toughened (clear, frosted or fluted) 8–10mm
Office partition Toughened; laminated for acoustics 10–12mm
Frameless glass door Toughened 10–12mm
Balcony & staircase railing Toughened or toughened-laminated 12mm / 13.52mm+
Table tops & shelves Toughened, edge polished 8–12mm
Shopfront & entrance Toughened or laminated 12mm+

General guidance only. Final thickness depends on panel size, height and wind load, and is confirmed at the site visit.

Toughened (tempered) glass is heat-treated, making it roughly 4 to 5 times stronger than normal (annealed) glass of the same thickness. If it breaks, it crumbles into small blunt granules instead of sharp shards, which is why it is used for doors, partitions, shower cubicles, and railings. It cannot be cut or drilled after toughening, so all holes and cut-outs are made before the toughening process.

As a general guide: 5–6mm for windows and wardrobe shutters, 8–10mm for shower enclosures, 10–12mm for office partitions and frameless doors, and 12mm toughened or 13.52mm laminated glass for balcony and staircase railings. The final thickness depends on panel size, height, and wind load, which we confirm during the site visit. See our thickness guide for more.

Frosted glass has a smooth, even matte finish (acid-etched or film) that blurs the view completely. Fluted (reeded) glass has vertical ribs that distort the view while letting more light and texture through. Fluted glass is popular for pooja doors, wardrobes, and partitions; frosted glass suits bathrooms and office privacy bands.

Wipe glass with a soft microfibre cloth and a mild, ammonia-free glass cleaner. In showers, squeegee the glass after each use to prevent hard-water stains. Avoid abrasive pads and acidic cleaners on hinges and handles, and keep sliding tracks free of dust and debris so doors glide smoothly.
